Privacy Policy

Last updated: 24 July 2026

Relay is a small, self-hosted chat service. It is not a commercial product: there are no ads, no analytics, no trackers, and nothing is sold or shared with advertisers. This page explains, in plain terms, what the software actually stores and who can see it.

Who runs this service

This is a private instance of Relay, run on a server controlled by its operator. Your data lives on that server. If you have questions about your data, or want your account or content removed, contact the person who runs this instance — the site administrator.

What is stored

  • Account details — your username, email address, display name, and optional bio and pronouns. Your password is stored only as an Argon2 hash; the plain password is never saved.
  • Google sign-in — if you sign in with Google, basic profile information (email address and name) is received from Google to create and identify your account. No other provider is used.
  • Messages — what you post in channels, along with timestamps, sender, reactions, mentions, edits, and thread relationships. Direct messages are stored too, but if you have turned on encryption they are stored only as scrambled text the server cannot read (see below). The surrounding details — who you messaged and when — are recorded either way.
  • Files you upload — attachments and images, stored on the server's disk.
  • Presence — whether you are online, when you are typing, and any "away" status. This is shown live to other users and is not kept as long-term history.
  • Moderation records — when an administrator or channel operator performs a privileged action (kick, ban, granting operator status, deleting a channel), the action, who did it, who it affected, and a short note are recorded for accountability.

Who can read your messages

Channel messages are readable by the operator. Traffic is encrypted with HTTPS in transit, but anything posted in a channel is stored in the database as ordinary text. The operator of this server — and anyone with access to the database or its backups — can read it. Do not post anything in a channel that would be harmful if read by the administrator.

Direct messages can be end-to-end encrypted. If you turn on encryption, your browser generates a private key that never leaves your device, and DMs between you and someone who has also turned it on are encrypted before they are sent. The server stores only scrambled text it has no way to unlock, so the operator genuinely cannot read those messages. A lock icon appears at the top of the conversation when this is active.

  • If either person hasn't enabled encryption, that conversation stays readable by the operator, just like a channel. The app tells you which state you're in.
  • Your private key is protected by a passphrase only you know. If you forget it, those messages cannot be recovered by anyone, including the administrator.
  • Encryption hides the contents of a message, not the fact that it happened. The server still records who messaged whom and when.
  • Messages sent before encryption was enabled remain stored as ordinary text.

File uploads are served from public links

Uploads are not access-controlled. Each uploaded file gets a hard-to-guess random address (for example /api/uploads/<random-id>). Anyone who has that link can open the file without logging in, whether or not they belong to the channel it was shared in. The link is not published or guessable, but it is the only thing protecting the file — so treat anything you upload to a channel as shareable.

Files sent in an encrypted DM are the exception. They are encrypted in your browser before they are uploaded, so the link leads only to scrambled bytes. The server stores no filename and no file type for them either — just an anonymous blob — and only the two of you can turn it back into a file.

Other details about uploads:

  • Photos have their location data removed. JPEG, PNG, and WebP images are re-encoded inside your browser before they are sent. That process discards embedded metadata, including EXIF GPS coordinates, so the original file with your location never leaves your device. If an image cannot be re-encoded, the upload is refused rather than sending the untouched original.
  • GIFs and documents are uploaded exactly as they are. They are not modified, so any metadata inside them — author names, location, editing history — is uploaded and served along with the file. Strip these yourself first if that matters to you.
  • Uploads are capped at 10 MB per file and 5 uploads per minute, and only images and common document formats are accepted.

Your IP address

Your IP address is used briefly to limit repeated failed sign-in attempts and to cap how many accounts can be created from one place. It is held in a short-lived cache and is not attached to your profile or message history. As with any website, the server software may record ordinary request metadata in its logs.

Controls you have

Open your profile to find these. Each one is enforced by the server, so switching it off actually stops the information being produced rather than just hiding it on your screen.

  • Typing indicators — stop others seeing when you're writing.
  • Online status — appear offline while still using Relay.
  • Direct messages — stop people starting new conversations with you.
  • Search visibility — hide yourself from user search. You stay visible in channels you're already in.

You can also change your password there. Doing so signs out every other device, so it's the thing to reach for if you think someone else has access to your account.

Third parties

  • GIF search — searches are relayed through this server rather than sent from your browser, so Giphy does not receive your IP address when you search.
  • Images hosted elsewhere — when a message contains a Giphy or Imgur image, your browser loads that image directly from those services in order to display it. This reveals your IP address and browser details to them, exactly as visiting any web page containing their images would.
  • Google — only if you choose to sign in with Google.

There are no other third parties, and no advertising or analytics services of any kind.

What is kept in your browser

Relay does not use tracking cookies. It stores two things locally in your browser: your sign-in tokens (so you stay logged in) and your list of ignored users (which never leaves your device). Logging out removes the tokens.

Deletion and retention

  • Deleting a message hides it immediately and erases it after 30 days. It disappears for everyone straight away, but the record stays in the database, marked as deleted, until a scheduled clean-up removes it for good. During that window an administrator could still recover it.
  • Uploaded files are removed once nothing points at them. When the message carrying a file is deleted, the file itself is deleted from the server by the same 30-day clean-up, and its link stops working.
  • Account information is kept until the operator removes it. Ask the administrator if you want your account and content deleted.

How your data is protected

Passwords are hashed with Argon2. Connections use HTTPS. Sign-in attempts are rate-limited, uploads are restricted by file type and served with headers that stop the browser from treating them as code, and every message is escaped when displayed so it cannot run scripts. No system is perfectly secure, and this one is maintained by a small operator — please keep that in mind when deciding what to share.
